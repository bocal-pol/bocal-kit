# Belgium — In-depth legal reference (DPO Toolkit)

> Content to verify against official sources before enforceable use. Sources last checked:
> 2026-11-16.

## Supervisory authority

**Autorité de protection des données (APD)** / *Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit (GBA)*.
Official site: [autoriteprotectiondonnees.be](https://www.autoriteprotectiondonnees.be).
DPO notification to the APD and prior consultation (art. 36 GDPR) via the APD's site.

## National transposition text

**Law of 30 July 2018 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of
personal data** — published in the Belgian Official Gazette, entered into force on 5 September
2018. Exercises the national margins left by GDPR, notably: minors, sensitive data, journalistic/
archiving processing, and public-sector-specific obligations. Also integrates the transposition of
the "police-justice" directive (EU 2016/680) into a single framework law rather than a separate
text.

## DPO appointment

Base criteria identical to GDPR art. 37 (public authority/body except courts acting in their
judicial capacity; core activity = regular and systematic large-scale monitoring of data
subjects; or core activity = large-scale processing of sensitive/criminal data). The law of
30/07/2018 clarifies the application of these criteria to the Belgian public sector — **confirm
against the consolidated text before concluding an obligation or an exemption**, secondary
sources consulted diverge on the exact scope for federal public bodies. The DPO is appointed
based on professional qualities, notably expertise in data protection law and practice.

## Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) — Belgian specifics

- The APD adopted, in force since April 1, 2019, a list of processing types for which a DPIA is
  mandatory (complementing the 3 cases of art. 35.3 GDPR), and a 9-criteria decision-support grid
  inspired by EDPB guidelines (WP248) — the same 9 criteria documented as the EU standard in
  `SKILL.md` Phase 4.
- The APD's internal rules of procedure provide for a possible six-monthly update of this list.
  **Never copy a dated version as being currently in force** — always point to
  [autoriteprotectiondonnees.be/publications](https://www.autoriteprotectiondonnees.be) for the
  current version before concluding that a processing activity isn't on it.
- Prior consultation of the APD (art. 36 GDPR): procedure and manual available on the APD's site
  ("user manual — prior DPIA consultation").

## Frequently relevant sectoral laws

- **Camera Law** (law of 21 March 2007 regulating the installation and use of surveillance
  cameras, revised since) — declaration to the local police, mandatory pictogram, limited
  purposes. Interacts with GDPR: a surveillance camera must comply with both regimes.
- **CCT n°68 of 16 June 1998** (National Labour Council) — protection of workers' privacy with
  regard to camera surveillance in the workplace. Distinguishes permanent/temporary surveillance;
  permanent surveillance specifically targeting a worker is not authorized under this CCT. 4
  limitative purposes (safety/health, property protection, production process control, work
  control).
- **CCT n°81** (National Labour Council) — control of networked electronic communications data in
  the workplace: principles of purpose, proportionality and transparency; private communications
  identified as such by the worker are excluded from control.
- Relevant as soon as a processing activity in the register (Phase 3) concerns staff monitoring —
  verify the current text with the National Labour Council (CNT/NAR) before applying.

## To verify before any enforceable use

This file summarizes a framework stable since 2018-2019, but three specific points remain to be
confirmed on the consolidated text or the APD's site before any high-stakes decision: (1) the
exact scope of the DPO obligation for the federal public sector, (2) the current content of the
APD's list of processing types subject to mandatory DPIA, (3) the current state of the Camera Law
after its successive revisions.
