# Quality checklist

## Mandatory points

- 16:9 horizontal format.
- Clean white background.
- The character is present.
- The character carries the central action, not just a decorative role.
- No composition copied from a past example — a new metaphor was invented for the current text.
- The image is strange, creative, interesting.
- Clean and restrained, the main subject doesn't exceed about 60% of the image.
- A single central structure per image.
- Few annotations, short, legible.
- Orange is used only for the main path or arrows.
- Red is used only for key points, problems, warnings, or results.
- Blue is used only for additional explanation, feedback, or system state.

## Failure signals

If any of these show up, regenerate or locally edit:

- A title like "Common pitfalls / Workflow / Architecture diagram / Roadmap" appears in the top
  left.
- The character looks like a mascot, sticker, or cute cartoon.
- The image looks like a PPT, course material, or formal flowchart.
- Too many elements, too many arrows, too many nodes.
- The text turns into a long explanatory block.
- The background has a paper texture, a shadow, a gradient, a beige tint, noise.
- Screenshot of a real UI or high-tech interface.
- Significant mistakes in the annotations or illegible text.
- The image is too rigid, without an offbeat metaphor.
- Too close a resemblance to a composition already used in `assets/examples/`.

## Iteration method

- Too plain: make the character the subject of the action, add a strange but coherent metaphor.
- Too complex: remove nodes, keep only one action and 3 to 5 short annotations.
- Too cute: emphasize "deadpan," "neutral and serious expression," "not cute," "not a mascot."
- Too PPT-like: remove title, frame, regular grid, and excess arrows, go back to a hand-drawn scene.
- Too close to a past example: keep the central idea, change the main object and the character's
  action.
- Incorrect text: prefer local editing; if there are too many mistakes, regenerate with fewer
  annotations.

## Delivery criterion

A quality image should first give the reader a sense of slight strangeness, then let them understand
the structure within a second.

If the first glance suggests a tutorial page rather than a strange product sketch on white paper,
the image isn't compliant.
